    Theory & how the selector works

    Tailoring first, defaults second

    MIL-STD-810 is a tailoring standard. Part One asks for a Life Cycle Environmental Profile (LCEP): every transport, storage and operating phase, its duration and its platform. Measured data from the real platform give the best test. The curves in this tool are the standard's defaults for when no measured data exist. They are deliberately enveloping, so expect them to be conservative.

    Random vibration and Grms

    A random vibration test is defined by an acceleration spectral density (ASD/PSD) W(f) in g²/Hz. Breakpoints are joined by straight lines on log-log axes, so each segment follows W = W₁(f/f₁)n with slope 10·log₁₀(W₂/W₁)/log₂(f₂/f₁) dB/octave. The overall rms acceleration is the square root of the area:

    Grms = √( ∫ W(f) df )

    Velocity and displacement follow by dividing by (2πf)² and (2πf)⁴ before integrating. Because random peaks reach about three times the rms value, shaker stroke is checked against 6σ peak-to-peak displacement and velocity against 3σ.

    Sine-on-random (helicopter)

    Rotor blades excite strong tones at the rotor speed 1P and the blade-passage frequency n·1P and its harmonics, over a low random background. Category 14 gives the random levels W₀ and W₁ and the peak sine amplitude A as a function of frequency for four materiel locations. The default 4 h per axis represents a 2500 flight-hour life; shorter lives scale linearly, down to a 0.5 h minimum, below which the levels are reduced with the fatigue relation instead.

    Time compression

    Annex A equates fatigue damage with an inverse power law (S–N curve): doubling the time at a lower stress gives the same damage as a shorter time at a higher stress. With exponent m, the PSD level scales with (T₁/T₂)2/m and a sine amplitude with (T₁/T₂)1/m.

    Shock

    Method 516.8 prefers a shock response spectrum (SRS) derived from measured data. Without data, it gives default SRS peak levels and effective durations, and terminal-peak sawtooth pulses as an alternative when the test equipment cannot reproduce an SRS. A pulse's velocity change ΔV = ½·A·T for a sawtooth; the equivalent free-fall height is ΔV²/2g.

    This tool is a starting point for a test plan, not a substitute for the standard. Confirm every level, duration and procedure against your copy of MIL-STD-810H and your contract's tailoring.

    MIL-STD-810 Vibration & Shock Selector

    Version 1.0 · by Eren Tozlu

    A free, browser-based tool that turns a product's life cycle into a starting test plan from the MIL-STD-810H defaults: Method 514.8 vibration profiles with their Grms, displacement and durations, and Method 516.8 shock and drop tests. All calculations run in your browser.

    Every breakpoint is transcribed from MIL-STD-810H and checked against the overall rms printed in the standard. The PSD calculator, shaker check and time-compression tools work with any profile.

    Made by Eren Tozlu, a mechanical design engineer with a background in rugged electronics packaging for defense, avionics and naval systems. More engineering tools at tools.ehcrea.com.

    How to use the selector

    New here? Pick an example from Load example life cycle… and change one thing at a time.

    Three steps

    1. Life cycle: tick each phase the item will see. Enter the distance, number of take-offs or flight hours; the test duration scales with it.
    2. Test item: mass and size select the transit drop; orientation decides per-axis curves or the envelope; the platform sets the shock levels.
    3. Read the plan: every test with its reference, Grms and duration. Click a row to see the curve, breakpoints and shaker check, and export the breakpoints as CSV for your vibration controller.

    Which profile when?

    Highway truck
    Commercial shipping on paved roads to a depot or customer.
    Wheeled vehicle
    Military tactical trucks and trailers on secondary roads and cross-country, as secured cargo.
    Loose cargo
    Unrestrained items bouncing on a truck bed (package tester, not a shaker).
    Jet cargo
    Air transport in a cargo aircraft; one minute per take-off.
    Helicopter
    Materiel installed in, or carried on, a helicopter: sine tones at the rotor frequencies over random.
    Minimum integrity
    Only when the environment is unknown. Prefer a tailored profile.
